**Purpose:** reduce the monthly squeeze by making education and training easier to compare, easier to plan around, and less likely to turn into a long paperwork mess.

This is not about arguing whether college is good or bad.
It is about making sure people can take a path forward without getting trapped by bad defaults, confusing rules, or weak accountability.

## The mechanism (plain language)

Education gets dangerous when the price is high, the payoff is unclear, and the household carries most of the risk.

Some people get a real ladder.
Some people get debt, confusion, and years of trying to untangle a system that was supposed to help them.

The problem is not just cost.
It is cost plus bad guidance, policy churn, and weak guardrails on programs and intermediaries.

## What good looks like

- clearer outcomes before enrollment
- safer repayment defaults after enrollment
- less paperwork and fewer late surprises
- more non-debt paths with real labor-market value
- simple public metrics so people can tell which paths are actually working

## Quick wins (pick 1-3, not all)

### Outcomes + honesty

1. **Publish simple local pathway comparisons** across four-year programs, community college, apprenticeships, and certifications.
2. **Ask schools to publish plain-language debt and earnings info by program**, not just campus-wide averages.
3. **Push for a one-page before-you-enroll summary** with cost, likely debt, completion rate, and typical first-year earnings.
4. **Flag programs with weak outcomes** instead of treating every credential like it means the same thing.

### Repayment + relief

5. **Push servicers or public-facing offices to use the safest repayment path by default** instead of steering people into the easiest short-term option.
6. **Ask for hard response timelines** on PSLF, borrower-defense, or other relief processing.
7. **Support midstream verification** so people do not find out in year ten that they were on the wrong track.
8. **Track transfer or servicing changes carefully** so chaos is not treated like a normal part of the system.

### Skills routes + local alternatives

9. **Build a local pathways dashboard** that compares cost, time, and likely outcomes across different routes.
10. **Push employers to drop degree screens where skills-based hiring would work just as well.**
11. **Back apprenticeship and certification routes** that have clear labor-market value.
12. **Ask public systems to treat community college and transfer routes as first-class options, not backup plans.**

## Scoreboard metrics (choose 3-5)

- median debt by program
- completion rate by program
- first-year earnings by pathway
- processing time for relief or verification requests
- number of borrowers caught in repeated forbearance cycles
- share of job postings using degree screens where skills routes could qualify
- transfer success rate across major local pathways

## Common failure modes

- The whole conversation turns into college yes or no, which is not the real issue.
- People get handed more choices but not better information.
- Relief exists on paper but takes so long it may as well not exist.
- Everybody talks about access and nobody talks about outcome quality.

## Bridge language

- "I am not anti-college. I am anti-trap."
- "If people are going to borrow, they should be able to plan around the terms."
- "Simple for borrowers. Strict for intermediaries."
- "We should be honest about which paths pay off and which ones mostly shift risk onto families."

## One Ask examples

- "Publish a local pathways dashboard by [date] that compares cost, completion, debt, and first-year earnings across four-year, two-year, and apprenticeship routes."
- "Set a 90-day processing standard for [PSLF / borrower-defense / state relief] requests and publish monthly performance."
- "Require plain-language pre-enrollment disclosures for any publicly supported program in this state/region."
- "Review degree requirements for public-sector roles and remove screens that are not actually needed for the job."
