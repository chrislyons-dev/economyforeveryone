**Purpose:** reduce the monthly squeeze by making care easier to use, easier to price, and less likely to turn into paperwork or debt.

This is not about solving healthcare in one meeting.
It is about finding one pressure point you can name clearly and move a little.

## The mechanism (plain language)

Healthcare gets expensive in more than one way.

Sometimes the problem is the bill.
Sometimes it is the denial.
Sometimes it is the hours lost to paperwork before care even starts.

The common thread is simple:
too much money and time get burned in the maze before they get to actual care.

That is why people feel squeezed even when spending keeps going up.

## What good looks like

- clearer prices before and after care
- fewer delays from prior auth and billing churn
- less medical debt damage after the fact
- less admin drag on clinicians and staff
- simple public metrics so people can tell whether anything is improving

## Quick wins (pick 1-3, not all)

### Price clarity + billing

1. **Publish hospital price-transparency compliance locally** and flag who is still hiding the ball.
2. **Require plain-language estimate tools** for common services and common patient questions.
3. **Push hospitals to publish financial-assistance rules plainly** so people are not learning about help after collections start.
4. **Ask for itemized bills by default** when charges are disputed or unusually high.

### Prior auth + delay

5. **Track prior-auth turnaround times** by payer and publish the pattern.
6. **Ask for denial-rate and overturn-rate reporting** so medical necessity is not a black box.
7. **Support gold-card style relief** for clinicians or services with a strong approval history.
8. **Push for clear response deadlines** with escalation when insurers miss them.

### Debt + harm reduction

9. **Back state or local medical-debt protections** so getting sick does not wreck credit for years.
10. **Require charity-care screening before collections** for nonprofit hospitals.
11. **Push for simple payment-plan standards** instead of one-size-fits-nobody demands.
12. **Ask for collections reporting**: how many accounts, how fast, and what protections exist.

### Admin drag + workforce strain

13. **Run an admin time-tax log** with clinicians, patients, or front-desk staff.
14. **Publish one minutes-lost measure** for prior auth, billing callbacks, or note churn.
15. **Pick one recurring paperwork loop** and ask for a 90-day pilot to reduce it.
16. **Use patient stories carefully** to show the human cost of delay without turning people into props.

## Scoreboard metrics (choose 3-5)

- prior auth turnaround time (median)
- prior auth denial rate and overturn rate
- price-transparency compliance rate for major local systems
- number of medical-debt accounts sent to collections
- share of patients screened for financial assistance before collections
- average time spent resolving a billing problem
- estimated clinician/admin time lost per week to one target workflow

## Common failure modes

- Everything gets dumped into healthcare is broken, which is true but useless.
- Meetings become complaint sessions with no ask, no owner, and no date.
- People ask for a giant reform when a local debt-protection or transparency fix is actually on the table.
- Hospitals and insurers point at each other until everybody gives up.

## Bridge language

- "I am not asking for perfection. I am asking for fewer tollbooths between a patient and care."
- "If the price is real, it should be explainable before the bill lands."
- "A delay can be a denial with better manners."
- "More spending should mean more care, not just more maze."

## One Ask examples

- "Publish local prior-auth denial and overturn rates by payer starting this quarter, and report them publicly every 90 days."
- "Require nonprofit hospitals in this state/county to screen for charity care before sending bills to collections."
- "Audit hospital price-transparency compliance locally and publish a simple scorecard by [date]."
- "Pilot one admin-drag reduction project in the next 90 days and report minutes saved, safety effects, and patient impact."
