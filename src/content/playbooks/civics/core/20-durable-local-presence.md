# Durable Local Presence (Show Up Before You Need It)

**Use when:** you want to build civic infrastructure that will matter later, not just react to the current crisis.
**Goal:** a face-to-face group that exists before the moment that needs it, in a community your side may have written off.

## Why this playbook exists

The organizing lesson from Hungary 2026: the volunteers who showed up on election day were the product of two years of in-person work in towns previous opposition politics had ignored. Organizations built in reaction to a crisis tend to arrive after the moment has passed.

The mechanism is permission, not persuasion. Many inside a failing movement already suspect the truth; what's missing is evidence that acting on it is safe. A visible, local, in-person group is that evidence. See [How Organizing Beat the Fear Machine](/blog/2026-05-31-how-organizing-beat-the-fear-machine/).

## Principles

- In person beats online. Presence is the product.
- Community work alongside political work. One without the other is less durable.
- Not dependent on any single candidate, election, or platform.
- Locally organized, not centrally controlled.
- The dignity test applies to everything (below).

## The build sequence

1. **Join before you found.** Find one existing face-to-face organization (neighborhood association, parent group, civic league) and show up for a month before deciding anything is missing.
2. **Pick the written-off ground.** Identify one or two communities in your area your side has historically ignored - rural if you're urban, suburban if you're rural. The goal is presence there before a crisis creates urgency, not flipping anyone this year.
3. **Do visible non-political work first.** Park cleanups, school supply drives, rides to appointments. The group's reputation for usefulness is what makes its political voice credible later.
4. **Add the political layer without switching identities.** Candidate forums, one-ask campaigns, meeting attendance: one thing the group does, not what the group is.
5. **Keep the rhythm boring.** Same time, same place, low commitment per session. Durability beats intensity.

## The dignity test

Run it on every event, flyer, and post:

> Does someone who voted the other way have a way to show up without being required to perform self-condemnation?

If not, you're building a community for people who already agree, not civic infrastructure. Related: [Dignity-Preserving Off-Ramps](/playbooks/civics/core/19-dignity-preserving-off-ramps/).

## What this is not

- Not opposition research or covert persuasion. If you'd be embarrassed to state your purpose out loud, change the purpose.
- Not a campaign field office. Campaigns borrow infrastructure; this playbook builds what they borrow.
- Not fast. Two years is the realistic scale. Start counting from today.

## Failure modes

- **Crisis-born groups** arrive late and dissolve when the crisis passes. Antidote: found in calm times, keep purpose broader than one fight.
- **Candidate capture:** the group becomes one politician's fan club and dies with their career. Antidote: no endorsements as identity; evaluate everyone against the same asks.
- **Purity spiral:** the most committed members raise the entry bar until only they can clear it. Antidote: the dignity test, enforced by whoever runs the meeting.

## Definition of done (per month)

1. One in-person gathering held or attended
2. One visible act of community usefulness
3. One new person greeted by name and invited back
4. One log entry: what happened, who showed up, next date on the calendar
