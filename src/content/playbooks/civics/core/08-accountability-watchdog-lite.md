# Accountability (Watchdog Lite)

**Use when:** something smells off and you want receipts, not rumors.  
**Goal:** find the real constraint: money, rules, contracts, staffing, enforcement.

## What to look at (in order)

1. Agendas + minutes (what they actually decided)
2. Budgets (what they funded)
3. Contracts (who got paid, for what, with what metrics)
4. Dashboards / reports (what they track, what they hide)
5. Audits / IG reports (when available)

## Questions that cut through fog

- “Who owns the next decision?”
- “What’s the timeline?”
- “What metric will you publish?”
- “What’s the enforcement mechanism?”
- “What happens if it fails?”

## Institutional independence audit

Use this variant when the question shifts from "is this decision clean?" to "can the referee survive the next government?"

For any oversight body in your jurisdiction (inspector general, redistricting commission, election authority, ethics office):

1. Who appoints its leadership, and can one party do it alone?
2. Who controls its budget - statute, or annual appropriations the current government controls?
3. What does removing its leadership require - cause and process, or a two-sentence email?
4. Can affected people and journalists inspect its work, or only receive its outputs?
5. Would the current design still be acceptable if political opponents ran it?

Full rubric: the six-question [Guardrail Stress Test](/levers/guardrail-stress-test/).
Copy-pasteable question list: [Institutional Independence Audit template](/playbooks/civics/templates/institutional-independence-audit/).

If you can't get a straight answer, that's information too - log it and ask the next official up the ladder.

## Escalation ladder (calm)

1. Ask directly (one ask + metric)
2. Ask in writing (creates record)
3. Bring it to a meeting (public record)
4. Contact oversight (inspector general, ethics office, ombuds)
5. Partner with a watchdog org (if needed)

## Accountability scoreboard

- Contract located? (Y/N)
- Performance metrics in the contract? (Y/N)
- Audit clause present? (Y/N)
- Dashboard or reporting requirement exists? (Y/N)
- Next procurement, renewal, or review date identified? (date)

## Watchouts

- Conspiracy brain: avoid it.
- Drama gravity: don’t feed it.
- Keep your claims tight.

## What success looks like

A published metric, an audit clause, a changed contract term, or a funded enforcement capacity increase.

## Definition of done

Leave with:

1. one documented constraint
2. one named owner
3. one escalation channel if needed
4. one log entry and next review date
