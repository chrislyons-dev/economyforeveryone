# Guardrail Stress Test

> Use this checklist to evaluate whether a specific reform, institution, or policy meaningfully reduces capture risk - or just rotates who holds the levers.

A reform earns the label "independent" if most of these are true:

1. **Executive independence** - the watchdog or institution is not controlled by the executive branch. Board selection involves civil society, cross-party agreement, or an independent body - not the government of the day.
2. **Funding durability** - the institution's budget survives government turnover. Funding is set by statute on a multi-year cycle, not subject to annual appropriations the current government controls.
3. **Judicial reviewability** - courts can review the institution's actions. People affected by its decisions have a contestation path that doesn't route back through the institution itself.
4. **Civil society and press access** - independent journalists and civic organizations can inspect the process, not just receive official outputs. Audit rights and document access exist in law.
5. **The opponent test** - the reform would still be acceptable to you if political opponents controlled it. If the answer is no, the design depends on good intentions, not structure.
6. **Tighter constraints for the next government** - the reform makes it harder for whoever comes next to abuse the institution, including the party that created the reform.

---

## How to use it

**For evaluation:** Run a specific institution or proposed reform through the six criteria. A reform that passes four or more is meaningfully designed. One that passes two or fewer is personnel change dressed as structural reform.

**For advocacy:** Use criteria 5 (the opponent test) and 6 (tighter constraints) as bridge language. They're ideologically neutral tests that work across political divides.

**For reading the case studies:** Each criterion maps to documented failure modes in [Guardrails After Victory](/case-studies/guardrails-after-victory/) - captured board selection (criterion 1), eliminated watchdog funding (criterion 2), ignored court rulings and unenforced removal protections (criterion 3), opaque media ownership and civil service reclassification (criterion 4), reforms designed for one side's hands (criterion 5), and Germany's constitutional court as the durable positive example (criterion 6).

---

## Where it came from

Distilled from Transparency International's framework for anti-corruption agency independence, applied across the cases in [Guardrails After Victory](/case-studies/guardrails-after-victory/). It extends to any institution whose independence is claimed but untested: courts, prosecutors, public broadcasters, redistricting commissions, inspectors general, anti-corruption offices.

The opponent test (criterion 5) is the most useful single criterion for public conversations. It reframes the question from "is this the good side?" to "is this a rule that will still work when the other side wins?" That framing cuts through partisan objections more reliably than the others.
